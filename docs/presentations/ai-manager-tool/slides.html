The problem nobody warned you about

You used to own your work.

What good looks like

Same tool, same meeting, two different asks

✗ what most people type
"summarise my meeting"

Three tidy paragraphs. No owners, no dates, nothing filed. You read it once, feel organised, and by Thursday it's gone. You've made a document, not a system.

✓ what to type instead
"Summarise the standup: decisions, action items with owners, flag anything I'm now waiting on, and file it."

Structured, owned, dated, stored. Next Tuesday it's the thing that writes your agenda for you.

The formula, every time: context  +  the shape of the output you want  +  where it should land.
The judgment that matters

Hand over the bookkeeping. Keep the people.

Be straight with your team about this

Four ways it will let you down

It invents specifics

A due date nobody agreed to, an owner nobody named. Read what it wrote before you act on it — and certainly before you forward it.

It files things wrong

It'll call a project a task. Because it's all plain text, you just fix the line — nothing is locked inside a system you can't open.

Nothing in, nothing out

The 11:00 moment came entirely from the 09:30 habit. Skip the capture and there's nothing to compound — it goes quiet inside a week.

Don't outsource the thinking

Drafting a performance conversation is where this stops saving you time and starts helping you avoid the job.

Monday morning

Don't build the system. Pick one stop in your day.

  • 1Start at 09:30. Summarise one meeting. Literally that, nothing else.
  • 2Do it for a week. Notice what you used to drop.
  • 3Add 11:00 second — it only pays once you have a few records to draw on.
  • 4Everything else on that timeline can wait a month.
why people fail at this

They try all five on Monday — and none of them by Thursday.

Pick the one that hurt most this week. One stop done properly beats five half-built.
