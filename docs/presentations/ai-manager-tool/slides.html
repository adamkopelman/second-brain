The premise

You don't have a knowledge problem.
You have a keeping-track problem.

  • →A manager's day is interrupt-driven: hallway asks, Slack pings, "quick" emails, decisions made and half-remembered.
  • →The work isn't hard to do — it's hard to hold in your head without dropping something.
  • →Every dropped ball costs trust: the thing you said you'd send, the person you were waiting on, the follow-up after the 1:1.
the shift

AI isn't here to think for you. It's here to hold the state so you can.

The rest of this session is one working system that does exactly that — and the six other plays you can copy from it.

Reframe

Chatbot  →  operator you delegate to

Using AI as a chatbot
  • ·One question, one answer, then gone
  • ·Remembers nothing tomorrow
  • ·You copy-paste the output somewhere
  • ·Answers live in a chat log you'll never find
Using AI as an operator
  • ✓Runs a named routine on command (a "skill")

The demo system

A "second brain" made of plain text

  • ▸An Obsidian vault — folders of markdown files. That's the whole database.
  • ▸Organised PARA-style: Inbox Projects Areas Resources Archive, plus People & Meetings.
  • ▸State lives in tags, not apps: #next #waiting and contexts like #computer #agenda.
  • ▸AI operates it through skills; you read it in Obsidian, a browser dashboard, or your phone.

Demo 1 · Capture

The two-second inbox

The whole point: never lose a thought to "I'll remember that." No app-switching, no deciding where it goes. Just say it.

  • 1Blurt anything — a task, an idea, a link, a name.
  • 2It lands in 00 Inbox with a timestamp.
  • 3You keep moving. Deciding comes later.
claude · capture
you ▸ /gtd-capture remind me to send Sam
the Q3 figures before Friday

claude ▸ Captured to 00 Inbox:
- [ ] Send Sam the Q3 figures
#next #agenda [[Sam Rivera]]
[due:: 2026-09-18]

# inbox: 1 item · nothing else to decide now
Demo 2 · Clarify

Empty the inbox with a decision tree

claude · process-inbox
you ▸ /gtd-process-inbox

claude ▸ 3 items. Item 1 of 3:
“Website feels slow lately”

# Is it actionable? yes
# One step or many? many → project

claude ▸ Filing as project Speed up the site
first #next: Profile the homepage
#computer [[Speed up the site]]
next ▸ item 2 of 3…

Clarifying is the step people skip. The operator walks the GTD decision tree for you and files each item.

  • ?Actionable? No → trash, someday, or reference.
  • ?One step? Yes → a next action with a context.
  • ?Many steps? → a project with a first action.
  • ?Someone else's? → #waiting on a person.
Demo 3 · Prioritise & engage

"What should I actually do right now?"

The list you need isn't everything — it's what fits this moment. Filter by where you are, how long you've got, and how much brain you have.

  • ▸Context — at your desk? #computer · on the move? #phone
  • ▸Time — "I have 15 minutes."
  • ▸Energy — "I'm fried, give me easy wins."
claude · next-actions
you ▸ what can I do at my computer in 15 min?

claude ▸ 4 #next actions fit:
□ Reply to the board email ~10m
□ Profile the homepage ~15m
□ Approve the design draft ~5m
□ Book the venue [due today]

# hidden: 12 tasks that don't fit right now
Demo 4 · Remember · the manager's killer app

Meeting → notes, decisions, action items

claude · summarize-meetings
you ▸ /gtd-summarize-meetings

claude ▸ Weekly sync — 2026-09-15
Decisions
• Ship v2 behind a flag first
Action items
- [ ] Adam: write rollout plan #next
- [ ] Waiting on Priya: legal sign-off
#waiting [[Priya]] [since:: 09-15]
# filed to Meetings/ + People notes updated

This is the play that sells the whole system. A recording (or transcript) becomes structured, filed follow-through — not a doc you never reopen.

  • ✓Decisions captured, so "what did we agree?" has an answer.
  • ✓Action items become real #next tasks with owners.
  • ✓Things you delegated become #waiting — you'll never chase blind again.
Demo 5 · Review & the dashboard

The weekly pass that keeps it honest

A second brain you don't trust is just another pile. The weekly review is the ritual that keeps trust — and the operator runs the checklist with you.

  • ▸/gtd-status — a 10-second brief the moment you sit down.
  • ▸/gtd-weekly-review — inbox to zero, stale projects, overdue waits.
  • ▸/gtd-dashboard — a shareable HTML view for any browser or phone.

Why this works when other tools didn't

Four principles worth stealing

Principle 01

You own the data

Plain markdown, not a proprietary app. No migration, no lock-in, no "the startup shut down."

Principle 02

Small verbs, not one big brain

Named skills you understand and trust, each doing one job — not a black box "assistant".

Principle 03

Human stays in the loop

AI drafts the filing; you approve. The system is auditable because you can read every file.

Principle 04

Context compounds

Every capture, meeting and review makes the next answer sharper. The value grows with use.

Keep it real

Four ways this goes wrong

Pitfall

Automating a broken process

If your review habit is chaos, AI just makes chaos faster. Fix the loop first, then hand it off.

Pitfall

Trusting without verifying

It can mis-file or invent a due date. Glance at what it wrote — especially before you act on it.

Pitfall

Feeding it the wrong things

Know what goes in a work vault vs. what stays out — secrets, HR specifics, anything you can't own.

Pitfall

Over-automating

The daily habit of capture & review is the point. Don't automate away the thinking you needed to do.

Do it yourself

Stand this up in 15 minutes

  • 1Grab the second-brain template and open the folder as an Obsidian vault.
  • 2Run /gtd-setup — it scaffolds the folders, templates and dashboard.
  • 3Capture for a day. Just /gtd-capture everything — don't organise yet.
  • 4Tomorrow, /gtd-process-inbox to empty it. That's the habit.
  • 5Friday, /gtd-weekly-review. Now it's a system, not a demo.
