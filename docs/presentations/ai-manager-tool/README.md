# The AI Chief of Staff — presentation kit

A session for **new team leaders of software teams**, built to sit inside a full day of soft-skills training. Runs at **45 minutes and cuts cleanly to 30**.

## The premise

Everything else in that day is about being with your people. This session is about **winning back the time to do it** — by handing the bookkeeping to an AI that runs your day with you.

Rather than teaching a productivity method, it walks through **one ordinary Tuesday** and shows exactly what gets handed over at five moments — and the one place it never does.

## What's in the kit

| File | What it is |
|------|------------|
| `slides.html` | The deck. One self-contained file — open in any browser, present fullscreen, works on a phone. 13 slides. |
| `speaker-notes.md` | Facilitator guide: **dual timing map (45 and 30 min)** with the cut slides marked, what to say per slide, prep checklist, Q&A. |
| `demo-script.md` | The five stops, with **live vs. shown** clearly separated. Only two commands are typed live. |
| `handout.md` | One page to keep: the delegation line, how to phrase an ask, the five stops. |

## The arc

The new-lead problem (you own everyone's follow-through) → the day at a glance → **five stops**: 08:40 inbox · 09:30 standup → action items · 11:00 prep from last time · 14:00 sprint review · 16:30 it drafts, you send → weak vs. strong ask → **the delegation line** → where it bites you → pick one stop → your first week.

## Live vs. shown

Only two demos run live, both vault-based and reliable:

- **09:30** — `/gtd-summarize-meetings`
- **11:00** — *"[meeting] in 10 minutes. What's still open from last time?"*

The inbox, sprint review and end-of-day stops are **shown as output**, because mail and tracker connectors aren't wired up in this vault. Two live commands is deliberate: very little can go wrong.

## Before you present

1. Read the prep checklist at the end of `speaker-notes.md`.
2. **Stage a prior meeting in `Meetings/`** — the 11:00 demo is the best moment in the session and it needs history to read. This is the step people forget.
3. Rehearse once end to end and screenshot every result as a network fallback.
4. Optional but worth ten minutes: run the three *shown* stops against your **own** data and swap those screenshots into slides 4, 7 and 8.

## Presenter notes

- **Never cut slide 6** (11:00 prep) — it's the moment that reframes the job — **or slide 10** (the delegation line), which is the session's whole argument.
- At 30 minutes: drop slide 3, merge 11 into 10, compress 4 and 7.
- Say the tie-in line in the opening and again on slide 10: *"Every other session today is about being with your people. This one is about winning back the time to do it."* It's what stops this feeling like a bolt-on tech talk.
