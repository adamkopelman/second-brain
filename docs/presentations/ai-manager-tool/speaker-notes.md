# Facilitator Guide — *The AI Chief of Staff*

One session inside a full day of tools for **new team leaders**. Every other session that day is soft skills; this is the one concrete/technical slot — treat it as a change of pace, not a tech lecture.

**Audience:** first-time leaders of software teams. Willing, technical, no systems of their own yet.
**Their real gap:** not motivation — they don't know *what good looks like*. What to hand over, and how to phrase it.
**Goal they leave with:** one stop in their day they'll actually run on Monday.
**Runs at 45 min. Cuts cleanly to 30** — the cut slides are marked below.

## The one line that ties it to the rest of the day

> *"Every other session today is about being with your people. This one is about winning back the time to do it."*

Say it in the opening, and again on slide 10. It's what stops this feeling like a bolt-on tech talk, and it makes the delegation line land: **hand over the bookkeeping, keep the people** — the right-hand column is exactly what the rest of the day teaches.

## Slide-by-slide

### 1 — Title (1:00)
Open with the tie-in line above. Then set the contract plainly: *"I'm not going to teach you a productivity system. I'm going to walk you through one ordinary Tuesday of mine and show you exactly what I hand off at five moments — and the one place I never do."*

### 2 — You own everyone's follow-through (2:30)
This is the empathy beat and it matters more than any demo. These people were promoted weeks ago and are quietly drowning. Name the shift: last month the job was finishing things; now it's making sure twenty things you're *not* doing happen. Ask for hands: *"Who's dropped a follow-up in the last two weeks?"* Every hand goes up. That's the enemy — not inefficiency, **dropped trust**.

### 3 — The day at a glance (1:30) *(cut at 30)*
Just orient them. Flag the **live** vs **shown** badges honestly — say out loud which ones you'll run and which you'll show output from. Then plant the pattern: *"Same four beats each time — the situation, what I ask, what comes back, and where I stay in charge."*

### 4 — 08:40 Inbox · **shown** (4:00)
Show the triage output. The key framing: *"I don't want it writing my email. I want it telling me which four of forty I can't ignore."* Land the lesson: **its attention for your judgment.** That trade is the through-line of the whole session — say it here so slides 5–8 just reinforce it.

### 5 — 09:30 Standup → action items · **LIVE** (5:00)
Run it. See `demo-script.md`. Narrate what the *ask* contains as you type it — decisions, owners, waiting-ons, file it. Then: *"The worst thing you can do with a meeting is remember it."* Emphasise that it captured who owes what, which is the thing they're currently losing.

### 6 — 11:00 Prep from last time · **LIVE** — *the money slide* (5:00)
The one that reframes the job. Run it against the meeting record from slide 5's demo (or a staged prior one). Let the "deferred twice" line breathe — that's the moment people sit forward, because every new lead has a decision that's been quietly deferred twice.

Then be rigorous about the dependency: **this slide only exists because of the 09:30 habit.** No capture, no payoff. That honesty is what makes the Monday ask credible.

### 7 — 14:00 Sprint review · **shown** (5:00)
Do the weak-vs-strong ask out loud before showing the result — it's your first taste of slide 9. Then land on the Dana bullet: *"'Dana reviewed 3× anyone else' is a people problem your board will never raise."* That's your explicit handoff into the rest of the day's content. Name it as such.

### 8 — 16:30 It drafts, you send · **shown** (5:00)
The ethics/judgment beat. Walk the four items and point at what it *refused* to touch — the review and the decision. Then the line: **"Pressing send is a promise from you to another person. That never gets delegated."** Also show the waiting-on list; for a new lead, "who owes me what" is worth the whole session.

### 9 — Weak vs strong (4:00)
The highest-teaching-rate slide. Read both asks aloud, in the same voice, so the only variable is the wording. Then give them the formula to write down: **context + the shape of the output + where it lands.** If they take one thing home, make it this.

### 10 — The delegation line (4:00)
The argument of the session. Walk both columns, then the tie-in: the right-hand column is the actual job, and it's what every other session today is about. This is where the session stops being about software.

### 11 — Where it bites you (3:00) *(merge into 10 at 30)*
Honesty buys credibility with technical people. Don't soften it. The fourth card — *don't outsource the thinking, especially feedback* — is the one to dwell on with new leads, because it's the most tempting misuse.

### 12 — Pick one stop (2:30)
The anti-overwhelm slide. They will try to build everything. Tell them the failure mode explicitly: *"all five on Monday, none by Thursday."* Push them to **09:30 only**, for one week.

### 13 — Your first week (2:30)
Concrete and small. Close on the tie-in line one last time, then hand back to the day.

## Swap in your real output — worth ten minutes

Slides 4, 7 and 8 ship with representative examples. Before the session, run those three against your **own** inbox and sprint board and screenshot the result. *"Here's what mine said this morning"* is dramatically more convincing to a room of new leads than any example I can write — and it removes any question of whether this really works.

Keep the illustrative version in your back pocket in case anything on screen is sensitive.

## Q&A — likely questions

- **"Does my company allow this?"** Depends where your vault lives and which model you use. Point them at whoever owns tooling — and note that the vault itself is just markdown on their machine.
- **"What about JIRA and email — is that live?"** Be straight: those two were shown as output, not run live. The connectors exist but need setting up. The meeting flows are the ones that work out of the box.
- **"Won't it get things wrong?"** Yes — slide 11. That's why it drafts and you send.
- **"Is this just note-taking with extra steps?"** The 11:00 slide is the answer. Notes you never reopen are overhead; a record that writes next week's agenda is leverage.
- **"How long until it's useful?"** About a week — you need a few records before the compounding starts.
- **"Should I tell my team I'm using it?"** Yes. Especially if it ever drafts something you send them.
